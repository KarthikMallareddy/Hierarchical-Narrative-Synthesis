**Hierarchical Attention Networks for Multi-Domain Document Classification: A Comparative Study**

*Authors: Alex Thompson¹, Maria Rodriguez², David Kim¹*
¹Stanford University, ²MIT, ³Google Research

**Abstract**

Document classification in multi-domain environments presents significant challenges due to varying vocabularies, writing styles, and structural patterns. This paper introduces a novel hierarchical attention mechanism that adaptively focuses on domain-specific features while maintaining cross-domain generalization capabilities. Our approach combines transformer-based encoders with domain-aware attention heads, achieving state-of-the-art performance across five benchmark datasets.

**1. Introduction**

The exponential growth of digital documents across diverse domains—from medical records to financial reports, legal documents to social media posts—necessitates robust classification systems capable of handling heterogeneous content. Traditional approaches often suffer from domain shift, where models trained on one domain perform poorly when applied to others.

Recent advances in transformer architectures have shown promise, but they typically require extensive domain-specific fine-tuning. Our contribution addresses this limitation through a hierarchical attention framework that learns domain-invariant representations while capturing domain-specific patterns.

**2. Methodology**

**2.1 Architecture Overview**
Our model consists of three main components:
- Base transformer encoder (BERT-large)
- Domain-aware attention module  
- Hierarchical classification head

**2.2 Hierarchical Attention Mechanism**
The attention mechanism operates at two levels:
- Token-level attention: Identifies important words within sentences
- Sentence-level attention: Weights sentence importance for document representation

Mathematical formulation:
α_ij = softmax(W_a * tanh(W_s * s_i + W_d * d_j))

Where s_i represents sentence embeddings and d_j represents domain embeddings.

**3. Experimental Setup**

**3.1 Datasets**
- Reuters-21578 (news articles): 10,788 documents, 90 categories
- PubMed Abstracts (medical): 14,523 documents, 15 categories  
- arXiv Papers (academic): 12,094 documents, 11 categories
- Legal Documents (law): 8,765 documents, 7 categories
- Social Media Posts (informal): 25,341 documents, 20 categories

**3.2 Baselines**
- BERT-base fine-tuned per domain
- Universal Sentence Encoder  
- Doc2Vec with SVM classification
- Traditional TF-IDF with Random Forest

**4. Results**

**4.1 Classification Performance**
Our hierarchical attention model achieves superior performance:
- Reuters-21578: 94.2% F1-score (+2.8% over BERT-base)
- PubMed: 91.7% F1-score (+3.1% over baseline)
- arXiv: 89.4% F1-score (+2.5% over baseline)
- Legal: 87.9% F1-score (+4.2% over baseline)
- Social Media: 82.3% F1-score (+1.9% over baseline)

**4.2 Cross-Domain Transfer**
Zero-shot transfer experiments demonstrate robust generalization:
- Medical→Legal: 73.4% accuracy
- News→Academic: 78.9% accuracy
- Academic→Social: 69.7% accuracy

**5. Analysis and Discussion**

The attention visualization reveals interesting patterns:
- Medical documents focus heavily on symptom and treatment terminology
- Legal documents emphasize procedural and regulatory language
- Social media content shows attention to sentiment-bearing words

Ablation studies confirm the importance of both hierarchical levels, with sentence-level attention contributing most significantly to performance gains.

**6. Conclusion**

Our hierarchical attention framework successfully addresses multi-domain document classification challenges. The model's ability to maintain performance across domains while capturing domain-specific features makes it suitable for real-world applications where document types vary significantly.

Future work will explore extension to multilingual settings and investigation of few-shot learning capabilities in low-resource domains.
